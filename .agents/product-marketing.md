## Product Overview
**One-liner:** We can help your child get the best education possible.
**What it does:** The Law Offices of Nicole Hodge Amey (LONHA) is a boutique California law firm providing legal representation for families of children with disabilities in disputes with public school districts — IEP disputes, due process hearings, discipline/expulsion matters, and Section 504/ADA discrimination claims.
**Product category:** Special-education / disability-rights legal representation (not a SaaS product — a local professional-services law firm with a single named attorney, no self-serve signup, tiers, or freemium model).
**Product type:** Professional services (law firm), marketing website only. Practice areas and contact form are the entire "product surface"; no app, no account system.
**Business model:** Paid initial consultation, then case representation on a retainer basis. Under IDEA, parents who prevail in due process may recover attorney fees from the school district (fee-shifting). No public pricing is published on the site beyond a general consultation-fee statement (per Phase 1 brief, Section 7.4) — exact numbers are not yet finalized/public.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** N/A (B2C — individual families, not companies).

**Use cases:**

- District proposes an IEP/placement change the parent disagrees with

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Something at school stopped working for the child, and the family doesn't know whether the fix requires a lawyer, an advocate, or another resource — and is up against a district that has its own lawyers.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Non-attorney IEP advocates ($50–$275/hr) can attend meetings and help navigate the district, but cannot represent the family in due process hearings, file legal complaints, or give legal advice
- Generic civil-rights firms/nonprofits don't specialize in IDEA/FAPE/IEP mechanics specifically
- No competing special-ed firm in the Bay Area/Kern County market offers full bilingual EN/ES content and representation (see Competitive Landscape)
**What it costs them:** Time-sensitive procedural deadlines missed, services the child is legally entitled to going undelivered, discipline records/placement decisions that are hard to reverse later.
**Emotional tension:** Parents already feel judged, dismissed, or manipulated by the school district's institutional authority by the time they're searching for a lawyer. They are wary of anything that reads as a sales tactic.

**Anti-persona:** Families whose situation is purely advocacy, coaching, or meeting-support with no legal dispute — these are better served by Amey Educational Advocates (AEA) and should be routed there, not treated as a LONHA lead.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** The district denied a service/evaluation, proposed an inadequate plan, or disciplined the child in a way connected to their disability — the parent is now in acute institutional conflict.
**Pull:** A named, 20+-year specialist attorney; full bilingual service; explicit geographic coverage; a stated 24-business-hour response time.
**Habit:** Families often default first to a non-attorney advocate (cheaper, more available, first point of contact) even when the matter has already outgrown what an advocate can do.
**Anxiety:** Cost of legal representation, fear that hiring a lawyer will escalate/antagonize the district, and distrust built up from feeling already manipulated by institutional authority — any copy that reads as a sales tactic will actively damage trust here.

**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| IEP | Individualized Education Program — the legal document defining a student's special-education services |
| IDEA | Individuals with Disabilities Education Act — the federal law guaranteeing special-education rights |
| FAPE | Free Appropriate Public Education — the guarantee at the center of most IEP disputes |
| Due process | The formal legal hearing process for resolving IEP/IDEA disputes |
| Section 504 | Rehabilitation Act provision protecting students whose disabilities aren't covered by an IEP |
